# PF-GOS Constitution v0.1

This document defines provisional constitutional constraints for PF-GOS. Implementations should be evaluated against these principles before optimization for scale, convenience, or adoption.

## Article 1 — Human agency
A person is not reducible to an identifier, credential set, score, wallet, or cryptographic proof. Technology must remain subordinate to human rights and lawful human judgment.

## Article 2 — Subsidiarity and autonomy
Decisions should be made by the smallest competent autonomous layer. Higher layers should not absorb authority merely because they possess greater technical or financial capacity.

## Article 3 — Distributed power
No actor should obtain permanent, unreviewable control over identity, assets, rules, data, adjudication, and infrastructure simultaneously.

## Article 4 — Minimum disclosure
A verifier should receive no more information than is reasonably necessary for the legitimate decision at hand. Proof of a condition should be preferred over disclosure of the underlying attribute when practical and safe.

## Article 5 — Institutional accountability
Privacy protects people; secrecy must not become a shield for institutional abuse. Public-power and fiduciary actions require proportionate auditability and accountability.

## Article 6 — Contestability
Material automated or protocol-mediated decisions affecting rights or significant interests must have an intelligible path to challenge, correction, and human review.

## Article 7 — Exit and portability
Participants should be able to leave a cluster and, where legally and technically possible, retain portable credentials, records, and legitimate rights without coercive lock-in.

## Article 8 — Recovery
Loss of a device, credential, or private key must not automatically mean irreversible loss of legal identity or fundamental rights. Recovery must resist both theft and permanent exclusion.

## Article 9 — Evolution
History may be tamper-evident while relationships and rules remain lawfully changeable. PF-GOS rejects the idea that governance should be permanently frozen merely because an implementation can make rules immutable.

## Article 10 — Intergenerational stewardship
Long-lived assets and institutions may survive individual lifetimes, but no generation may create permanently unaccountable power over future generations. Succession mechanisms must include review and amendment paths.

## Article 11 — Anti-discrimination and anti-scoring
PF-GOS must not become a universal social-credit system. Proof requirements must be necessary, proportionate, reviewable, and constrained against discriminatory proxy use.

## Article 12 — Forkability
The protocol, constitutional ideas, and open specifications should remain practically forkable so that failure or capture of one institution does not terminate the broader governance experiment.

## Article 13 — Security by minimization
Do not collect, centralize, retain, or replicate sensitive information merely because it is technically possible. Minimize both attack surface and institutional temptation.

## Article 14 — Lawful interface
Cryptographic execution does not supersede applicable law, courts, due process, taxation, fiduciary obligations, or public accountability. Legal and technical state must have explicit reconciliation mechanisms.

## Article 15 — Proof has limits
Not everything of human value is formally provable. Care, trust, dignity, art, moral judgment, and relationships must not be forced into machine-verifiable metrics simply to fit the system.

---

**Amendment principle:** constitutional changes should require a more demanding process than ordinary implementation changes, with public rationale, explicit dissent capture, and a defined review period. The exact mechanism is an open design issue for v0.2.
